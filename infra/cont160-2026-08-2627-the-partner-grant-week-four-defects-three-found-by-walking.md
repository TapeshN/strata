A number mismatch is not a defect until you check whether one number groups the other.

Before calling two numbers a contradiction, test the grouping hypothesis first: does the smaller set SUM to the larger? A disagreement and a roll-up look identical until you do the arithmetic.

A "these numbers disagree" claim requires the sum shown, not asserted.

A helper is only as safe as its callers — enumerate them before calling it a floor.

When designating any function as the enforcement floor, enumerate its callers FIRST and state the count. "Enforce at X" is a claim about coverage, not about X.

A floor claim names its callers. The list growing under contact is itself evidence the DB constraint, not the helper, is the real floor.

Credential scope follows the REPOSITORY, not the path.

WHERE a document lives determines WHO can read everything beside it. Two instances in one day (design library, partner docs) make it a rule: content a partner or a service reads must not sit in the repo holding your most sensitive material.

Before adding any read credential, ask what else that scope can reach.

Built-but-unwired is a pattern, not bad luck.

A capability is not delivered until something in the product calls it. An empty column on a real screen is the witness that it is not.

For any new service, name its first consumer in the same brief. No consumer named = not ready to build.

Traversal finds what audits miss.

A page audit answers "does this look right". A task walk answers "can someone who did not build this do the thing". Only the second finds a feature that works, is tested, and is filed under a name that contradicts it.

Before re-cutting any surface, walk its tasks cold.

A mutable label can never be an authority.

If a caller can supply it, it is input — not identity. Enforcement reads durable state or a derived fact, never a template label.

Ask of any enforcement field: can the actor being restricted change it?

A permanently-red main normalises ignoring CI.

A standing red is a debt with compounding interest: each correct bypass makes the next one easier and the eventual real failure quieter. Fixing it is cheaper than the judgement it costs on every subsequent merge.

A red that survives two merges gets an owner.
